NTSB Highway Accident Brief HAB-18/10
Fatal Pedestrian Collision with Transit Bus, New York City, New York, October 21, 2016
Accident Number: HWY17SH006. Adopted: July 31, 2018.
Source: https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/HAB1810.pdf
Accessed: 2026-09-19

Vehicle: 2006 Motor Coach Industries transit bus. Driver: 63-year-old male. Pedestrian: 58-year-
old female. Fatalities: 1. Location: Water/State Street and Whitehall Street, New York City.

CRASH DESCRIPTION
About 5:30 p.m. on Friday, October 21, 2016, a transit bus operated by the New York City
Metropolitan Transportation Authority (MTA) was traveling southwest on Water Street in Lower
Manhattan. The bus entered the intersection with Whitehall Street on a green signal but did not
clear it before the light turned red, stopping behind a line of vehicles one block west and blocking
part of the crosswalk. A 58-year-old female pedestrian, talking on her cell phone and walking
slower than her group, entered the crosswalk on a WALK signal as other pedestrians passed safely
in front of the stopped bus. As she walked in front of the bus, the traffic signal one block west
turned green and southwestbound vehicles -- including the bus -- began moving, even though the
signal at Whitehall Street (governing her crossing) was still red and she still had the WALK signal.
The bus struck her with the right side of its front bumper, knocked her down, ran over her, and
dragged her underneath for about half a mile before witnesses at a later intersection alerted the
driver and police. She died of multiple blunt force trauma; postcrash toxicology was negative for
alcohol and other drugs. Weather: 66.9 degrees F, calm winds, cloudy, still daylight (about 30
minutes before a 6:06 p.m. sunset).

CRASH LOCATION
An urban, heavily trafficked intersection in Manhattan's Financial District, near the Staten Island
Ferry terminal -- thousands of pedestrians use the area daily. Posted speed limit 25 mph. A city
Department of Transportation traffic safety survey found only 3 pedestrian collisions at this
intersection between 2011 and 2015, only one involving a pedestrian in a crosswalk on a green
signal, and none fatal.

DRIVER
Hired by MTA in 1977, held a New York Class B CDL with passenger endorsement. His most
recent annual physical (September 6, 2016) found him medically qualified with normal eyesight;
he did not report any medical conditions despite the exam listing several prescription
medications (in a 2014 exam he had reported high blood pressure only). He failed to disclose a
personal-vehicle accident from the previous 12 months during MTA's October 7, 2016 annual
employment review. Since April 2014, he had been involved in five transit bus crashes before this
fatal one (at fault in at least two), had four citizen reckless-driving complaints filed against him,
was cited twice for speeding in an MTA bus, and had undergone three mandated supervisor
observations because of his driving record. Postcrash toxicology was negative for drugs; he was
not tested for alcohol because more than 8 hours had elapsed (he was hospitalized for emotional
distress after the crash).

VEHICLE
A 2006 Motor Coach Industries DL4500 bus (max 61 passengers), inspected 3 weeks before the
crash (October 3) with all repairs completed. Postcrash inspection found no mechanical defects;
the service brake functioned within manufacturer and MTA guidelines. An NTSB investigator
confirmed that, given the driver's seat design (upright and forward), the driver could have seen the
pedestrian in front of the bus had he scanned the crosswalk before accelerating.

MTA SYSTEM-WIDE CONTEXT
The MTA employs over 12,700 transit bus operators with at least 5,600 buses on the road at any
time, and runs a 6-week initial training program. An internal MTA crash analysis found that
operators with 3-5 years of service were more likely to be involved in crashes, leading to the
"TOPS" (Transitional Operators Program) retraining initiative for that group. The MTA also runs
a "ghost rider" program (random unannounced rides by staff to spot unsafe driving) -- over 16,000
random rides had been taken between November 2014 and this crash, though the driver's own
random rides all predated November 2014. At the time of this crash, MTA had been involved in
62 fatal pedestrian crashes since 2004: 8 in 2015, and 5 in 2016 before this one.

VEHICLE-BASED SAFETY COUNTERMEASURES
This bus was not equipped with an "S-1 Gard" system (a deflector installed in front of the rear
wheels to push a fallen body away from them, reducing injury severity if someone falls under a
bus). MTA had examined this system but decided prevention was preferable, and was instead
testing two other systems: Clever Devices' "TurnWarning" system (senses a turn and audibly
warns nearby pedestrians a bus is turning, logging date/time/location of each announcement), and
a Rosco/Mobileye system that scans for pedestrians/cyclists and warns the driver audibly and
visually, with planned expansion to automatic deceleration/collision-avoidance braking.

APPLICABLE LAW
Under NY Vehicle and Traffic Law Article 24 Section 1112, pedestrians facing a WALK signal
have the right of way. Article 26 Section 1146 requires drivers to exercise "due care" to avoid
colliding with pedestrians/cyclists/animals, with penalties (including imprisonment) for violations
causing injury. NYC's "Right of Way Law" (Administrative Code 19-190, enacted August 2014 as
part of Vision Zero) makes it a misdemeanor for a driver to kill or seriously injure a pedestrian or
cyclist through failure to yield or exercise due care.

PROBABLE CAUSE
The National Transportation Safety Board determines that the probable cause of the crash was the
failure of the bus driver to check for pedestrians in the crosswalk immediately in front of the bus
before he accelerated from a stopped position.

BOARD MEMBER CONCURRING STATEMENT
Member Earl F. Weener concurred with the probable cause but separately noted the pedestrian was
using a mobile phone while crossing, citing National Safety Council guidance against crossing
while using an electronic device and noting some jurisdictions (Honolulu in 2017) have passed
laws against pedestrians viewing cell phones while crossing streets.
