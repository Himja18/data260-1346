S&S-30 SECURITY CONFIGURATION -- WORKED EXAMPLES (verbatim from manual)
Example: A transit agency operates Demand Response (DR) and Bus (MB) service and contracts
with a security company providing two personnel to patrol the transit center 8 hours a day, 5
days a week, each working a 4-hour shift (20 hours/week total per person). The MB/Directly
Operated mode uses the transit center; DR/DO does not. Solution: the agency reports 1.0 FTE
Primary Security Personnel for MB/DO and 0 for DR/DO.

Example: A transit agency uses four full-time employees plus one-third of an additional
employee's time to provide security on its buses. Solution: the agency reports 4.3 FTE for
"Number of Primary Security Personnel."

Example: A transit agency operates both Motor Bus and Light Rail. It has 12 security guards
patrolling non-revenue facilities (administrative buildings) and contracts an additional five
guards to respond only to events on light rail vehicles (17 full-time guards total). It also uses
Non-Contracted Local Police for the MB mode. There are 400 MB and 100 LR employees
working in the administrative buildings. Solution for MB: 400/500 = 0.8; 0.8 x 12 = 9.6 FTE
Primary Security Personnel ("Transit agency security force"), 0 Secondary Personnel (secondary
configuration is "Local police non-contracted"). Solution for LR: 5 FTE Primary ("Contracted
security force"); 100/500 = 0.2; 0.2 x 12 = 2.4 Secondary Personnel ("Transit agency security
force"), for a Total Number of Security Personnel of 7.4 (2.4 + 5).

Example: A transit agency operates LR and MB service with a full-time dedicated transit police
force of 30 officers covering both modes, plus a contract with local police for 10 off-duty
officers working 4-hour shifts during peak periods. This works out to 15 full-time security
personnel per mode (30/2) plus 2.5 part-time security personnel per mode (10 part-time = 5
full-time equivalents, divided by 2 modes). Solution: for both LR and MB S&S-30 forms, report
15 for Primary Security Personnel ("Dedicated transit police force") and 2.5 for Secondary
Security Personnel ("Off-duty police officers").

PERSONAL SECURITY EVENTS (verbatim category list and definitions)
Personal Security Events include: Assault (including Assault on a Transit Worker), Attempted
Suicide, Suicide, Homicide, Motor Vehicle Theft, Robbery, Rape, Larceny/Theft, and Other
Personal Security Events.

A reportable Personal Security Event is one resulting in an injury requiring transport away from
the scene for medical attention, a serious injury (rail mode), a fatality, property damage of
$25,000 or more (non-rail), substantial damage (rail mode), or an evacuation for life-safety
reasons or to the rail right-of-way (rail mode).

Important clarification: If two events occur at the same time and place (concurrent events), the
agency reports the event type that occurred first -- with the exception that a collision resulting
from a security event (assault, homicide, suicide, or attempted suicide by impact with a transit
vehicle) is always reported as a Collision, not as the underlying security event type.

Major and non-major thresholds occurring in a single incident are reported as TWO separate
events. Example: a transit bus strikes a private vehicle resulting in a tow-away, and the driver of
the private vehicle assaults the bus operator, but the operator is not transported for medical
attention. The agency reports the collision on the S&S-40 (it met the tow-away threshold) AND
separately reports the non-major assault on the operator on the S&S-50 (since the assault itself
did not meet any major threshold).

OTHER SAFETY EVENTS
Other Safety Events are safety events that are NOT collisions, fires, security events, hazardous
material spills, acts of God, or derailments. Examples include slips, trips, falls, smoke events (no
fire suppression), fumes, runaway trains (when not resulting in a collision or derailment), and
electric shock. These are ONLY reported as a Major Event if they meet the fatality, evacuation,
or property damage threshold, OR result in two or more injured persons. An Other Safety Event
resulting in exactly one person immediately transported from the scene for medical attention
(and meeting no other major threshold) is instead reported on the Non-Major Summary Report
(S&S-50), not the S&S-40.

S&S-50: NON-MAJOR MONTHLY SUMMARY REPORT (verbatim overview)
The Non-Major Monthly Summary Report (S&S-50) captures safety events that meet the NTD
injury threshold (one person transported for medical attention, but not meeting any major
threshold) in addition to non-major fires and non-major assaults on a transit worker. Notably,
assaults on a transit worker do NOT require an injury to be reportable on the S&S-50 -- an
assault with zero injury (e.g., spitting, verbal threat accompanied by physical contact) is still
reportable there. Transit agencies must submit an S&S-50 for each mode and type of service
every month, even if no event occurred that month (a "zero report").

Multi-Agency Facilities: When multiple transit agencies share a facility (e.g., a joint bus/rail
station), each agency reports events involving its own vehicles/personnel separately; the facility
itself is not double-counted as belonging to only one agency for reporting purposes.

NON-MAJOR ASSAULTS ON TRANSIT WORKERS
This is a distinct sub-category tracked on the S&S-50 specifically because assaults on transit
workers (bus operators, train operators, station agents, etc.) are considered significant even at low
severity, given their impact on workforce safety and morale, independent of whether the incident
also caused a physical injury requiring medical transport.

NUMBER OF OTHER SAFETY INCIDENTS / NUMBER OF NON-MAJOR FIRES
The S&S-50 also captures a simple count of "Other Safety Incidents" (single-injury Other Safety
Events not meeting a major threshold) and "Non-Major Fires" (suppressed fires not meeting any
major threshold) for the reporting month, without the detailed event-by-event breakdown required
on the S&S-40.
