INTRODUCTION: THE NATIONAL TRANSIT DATABASE
The National Transit Database (NTD) was established in 1974 and has evolved into the
Nation's primary source of information and statistics on transit systems. The Federal Transit
Administration (FTA) provides billions of dollars each year in transit assistance, in part based on
the data collected through the NTD.

WHO REPORTS
Safety and Security (S&S) reporters are those transit agencies that submit a Full Report to the
NTD, also called "Full Reporters." Transit agencies reporting as Reduced Reporters or Rural
Reporters do not report S&S data on the reporting forms on the Safety & Security tab; instead,
they report S&S data on their Annual Report. FTA assigns an S&S analyst to each transit agency.

REPORTING OVERVIEW (Exhibit 1)
- Chief Executive Officer Certification (S&S-20): All Full Reporters.
- Security Configuration (S&S-30): All Full Reporters.
- Major Event Report (S&S-40, safety events): All Full Reporters, except Commuter Rail (CR)
  and Alaska Railroad (AR) modes.
- Major Event Report (S&S-40, security events): All Full Reporters.
- Non-Major Monthly Summary Report (S&S-50): All Full Reporters. CR and AR modes must
  only report non-major assaults on a transit worker.

Transit agencies must submit one S&S-40 report for each major event that meets NTD reporting
thresholds. Transit agencies summarize less severe events on the Non-Major Monthly Summary
Report (S&S-50), which captures safety events that meet the NTD injury threshold plus non-
major fires and non-major assaults on a transit worker. Assaults on a transit worker do not
require an injury to be reportable on the S&S-50.

WHEN TO REPORT
The Major Event Report (S&S-40) is due no later than 30 days after the date of the event. The
Security Configuration (S&S-30) is due by February 28, 2026. The Non-Major Summary form
(S&S-50) must be submitted for each mode and type of service (TOS) every month, even if no
event occurs, with monthly due dates running from February 28 (for January) through January
31 of the following year (for December).

S&S-20: SAFETY AND SECURITY CEO CERTIFICATION
The Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of each transit agency must submit an annual Safety and
Security CEO Certification (S&S-20), confirming that all S&S data submitted are accurate for
the calendar year. It automatically tallies the number of events, injuries, and fatalities reported
across Major Event forms, plus events, injuries, fires, and assaults on transit workers from the
Non-Major Summary form. The agency's CEO must complete the S&S-20 by the end of February
2026.

S&S-30: SECURITY CONFIGURATION
The Security Configuration (S&S-30) indicates the number and type of personnel that respond to
security events or provide security on a transit agency's property. There are seven personnel
type selections: Dedicated transit police force; Dedicated (transit) unit of local police;
Contracted local law enforcement; Transit agency security force; Contracted security force;
Off-duty police officers; and Use of local police (non-contracted). Security personnel are
reported as "Primary" (routinely patrol or respond to events on transit property) and/or
"Secondary" (occasionally respond or assist the primary force) configurations. Agencies report
personnel in terms of Full-Time Equivalents (FTE), where one FTE equals one full-time
employee typically working 2,080 hours per year.

S&S-40: MAJOR EVENT REPORT
The Major Event Report (S&S-40) captures detailed information on severe safety and security
events. Agencies must complete one S&S-40 per reportable event, regardless of how many
thresholds an event meets.

A reportable event must: occur at a transit revenue facility, maintenance facility, or rail yard;
occur on transit right-of-way or infrastructure; occur during a transit-related maintenance
activity; or involve a transit revenue vehicle.

NOT reportable: events off transit property where the person/vehicle ends up on transit property
afterward; occupational safety events in administrative buildings; events during
engineering/construction phases of a new or extended rail system (unless involving transit
operations, testing, or transit-related maintenance); deaths confirmed to result from illness, drug
overdose, or natural causes (a fatal heart attack in a transit facility is NOT reportable; deaths of
undetermined cause in a rail right-of-way that may be the result of collision or electrocution ARE
reportable); events at bus stops/shelters not on transit-owned property (unless involving a transit
vehicle or a person boarding/alighting); collisions during travel to/from maintenance in a service
vehicle; and collisions involving a supervisor car or other non-revenue vehicle on public roads.

PREDOMINANT USE RULE: When an event affects more than one mode, the agency reports the
event under only one mode. If rail and non-rail are both involved, report under the rail mode. If
two rail modes or two non-rail modes, choose based on predominant use (typically measured by
passenger volume).

MAJOR EVENT THRESHOLDS (Exhibit 5)
Fatality: Confirmed within 30 days; includes suicides. Applies to both rail and non-rail modes
identically.

Injury (non-rail/ferry): Immediate transport away from the scene for medical attention for one or
more persons.
Injury (rail): Non-serious (transport for medical attention) OR Serious injury, which is any of:
hospitalization for more than 48 hours within 7 days of the event; a fracture of any bone (except
simple fractures of fingers, toes, or nose); severe hemorrhages or nerve/muscle/tendon damage;
internal organ involvement; or second/third-degree burns or burns affecting more than 5 percent
of body surface.

Property damage (non-rail): Estimated property damage equal to or exceeding $25,000.
Substantial damage (rail): Damage to any involved vehicles, facilities, equipment, rolling stock,
or infrastructure that (a) disrupts operations of the rail transit agency AND (b) adversely affects
structural strength, performance, or operating characteristics such that it requires towing, rescue,
on-site maintenance, or immediate removal prior to safe operation. Excludes cracked windows,
dents/bends/small punctures, broken lights/mirrors, or removal from service for minor
repair/testing.

Evacuation: A condition where persons depart transit vehicles/facilities for life-safety reasons
(fire, smoke, noxious fumes, fuel leak, electrical hazard, bomb threat, suspicious item, or other
imminent hazard). Rail modes must also report all evacuations to controlled rail right-of-way.
Self-evacuations (without direction from transit personnel) are NOT automatically reportable.
Evacuations due to mechanical failure are not reported unless onto the right-of-way or there was
imminent danger.

Derailment (rail only): Any derailment of a rail transit revenue vehicle or non-revenue service
vehicle, including both mainline and yard derailments. If caused by a collision, report as a
collision instead and note the derailed vehicles on the "Rail Transit Train Involved" form.

Collisions Involving Tow-Aways: All collisions involving revenue roadway vehicles where one or
more motor vehicles required towing, regardless of severity. Applies to motor vehicles only, not
rail cars or ferry boats.

Automatically Reportable Rail Collisions (regardless of other thresholds):
- Rail Grade Crossing or Intersection Collisions (any place a rail guideway used for transit
  operations is crossed at grade by a road or path, including station crosswalks; excludes parking
  lot entrances/driveways).
- Rail Collisions with an Individual (transit vehicle must be in motion; a stopped-vehicle impact
  with a person is reported as a fall on the Non-Major Monthly Summary form instead).
- Rail Collisions with Another Rail Vehicle (any collision between a transit revenue train and
  another revenue train, a revenue train and a non-revenue rail service vehicle, or two non-revenue
  rail service vehicles).

Runaway Train (rail only, revenue vehicles): A train moving and no longer under control of a
driver (whether or not the operator is physically aboard). Reportable regardless of any other
threshold, under whichever event type resulted (Collision, Derailment, or Other Safety Event).

EVENT TYPES (Exhibit 11)
Safety Events: Collisions (incl. suicide/attempted suicide/assault/homicide), Fires (suppression),
Derailments, Hazardous Material Spills, Acts of God, Other Safety Events.
System Security Events: Bomb Threat/Bombing, Chemical/Biological/Radiological/Nuclear
Release, Arson, Sabotage, Burglary, Vandalism, Hijacking, Cyber Security Events, Suspicious
Package, Other System Security Events (e.g., projectiles thrown at vehicles).
Personal Security Events: Assault (incl. on a Transit Worker), Attempted Suicide, Suicide,
Homicide, Motor Vehicle Theft, Robbery, Rape, Larceny/Theft, Other Personal Security Events.

COLLISIONS
A collision is a vehicle or vessel accident involving impact of a transit vehicle/vessel with another
vehicle, a person, an animal, an object, a rail vehicle, a vessel, or a dock.
Reportable non-rail collision: results in injury requiring transport, a fatality, a life-safety
evacuation, property damage of $25,000+, or towing of the transit or non-transit vehicle.
Reportable rail collision: results in a transport-requiring injury, a serious injury, a fatality, a
life-safety/right-of-way evacuation, substantial damage, a runaway train, occurs at a grade
crossing/intersection, or involves collision with a person or another rail vehicle.

Suicides, attempted suicides, assaults (incl. on transit workers), and homicides by impact with a
transit vehicle are reported AS COLLISIONS, not as security events. An attempted suicide is
self-inflicted harm where death does not occur but intent was fatal; intent must be established by
a third party (police reports, security personnel reports, or eyewitness statements) -- self-report
alone is not sufficient. An assault is an attack by one person on another without lawful authority.
A homicide includes murder/non-negligent manslaughter (willful killing) and negligent
manslaughter (killing through gross negligence).

FIRES
A fire event is reportable only if it was suppressed in some manner AND meets a reportable
event threshold (injury, serious injury for rail, fatality, $25,000+ property damage, substantial
damage for rail, or life-safety/right-of-way evacuation). Arson does NOT qualify as a fire event
and is reported as a security event instead. A smoke-only event with no fire suppression is not
reported as a fire; if it caused a life-safety evacuation, it is reported as an Other Safety Event.

HAZARDOUS MATERIAL SPILLS
A hazardous material spill is the release of any amount of material creating imminent danger to
life, health, or the environment, requiring special cleanup attention. Leaks of oil, power steering
fluid, or brake fluid from a transit vehicle are explicitly NOT considered sufficient quantity to
constitute a reportable spill.

ACTS OF GOD
Natural, unavoidable catastrophes interrupting the expected course of events: earthquakes,
floods, hurricanes, tornadoes, other high winds, lightning, snow storms, and ice storms.
Reportable only if meeting an injury/serious injury/fatality/property damage/substantial
damage/evacuation threshold.

SECURITY EVENTS -- KEY DEFINITIONS
Bomb Threat: A credible written or oral communication threatening use of an explosive/
incendiary device to disrupt transit service or create a public emergency.
Bombing: Unlawful, intentional delivery/placement/discharge/detonation of an explosive device
against transit property intending death, injury, or extensive property damage.
Arson: Unlawfully and intentionally damaging or attempting to damage property by fire or
incendiary device.
Hijacking: Unlawfully seizing control of a transit vehicle by force, threat, or intimidation, with
intent to detain persons aboard against their will, transport them somewhere other than the next
scheduled stop, or cause the vehicle to deviate from schedule.
Sabotage: Deliberate destruction of transit property or slowing of operations by employees
intending to damage the agency's business or economic condition.
Cyber: An event targeting transit facilities, personnel, information, or computer/
telecommunications systems, including denial/disruption of services (especially train control
systems), unauthorized monitoring, unauthorized disclosure of proprietary/classified information,
or unauthorized modification/destruction of programming code or databases.
