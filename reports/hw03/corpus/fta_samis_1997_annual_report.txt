ABSTRACT
The Safety Management Information Statistics (SAMIS) 1997 Annual Report is a compilation
and analysis of transit accident, casualty, and crime statistics reported under the Federal Transit
Administration's National Transit Database Reporting System by FTA-funded transit systems in
the United States during 1997. In its eighth year of publication, SAMIS provides uniformly
collected comprehensive safety and security data from all recipients or beneficiaries of Urbanized
Area Formula funds.

REPORTING SCOPE
Transit safety data are collected in four basic categories: Collisions, Derailments/Buses going off
road, Personal Casualties, and Fires. All transit agencies subject to NTD reporting must report
safety data. Transit security data are modeled after the FBI's Uniform Crime Reporting System,
using standardized offense definitions (Part I crime index offenses and Part II arrests). Transit
agencies serving urbanized areas (UZAs) with populations of 200,000 or more are required to
report security data. SAMIS reports property damage when it exceeds $1,000 (a lower threshold
than many other transportation industries use).

GLOSSARY OF KEY TERMS (SAFETY)
Vehicle Accident: An incident involving a moving vehicle, including collisions with another
vehicle, object, or person (except suicides) and derailment/leaving the roadway.
Collision with Vehicle/Object/People: An incident in which a transit vehicle strikes or is struck
by another vehicle, an obstacle (e.g., building, utility pole), or a person; reported if it results in
death, injury, or property damage over $1,000. Collisions with people exclude suicide attempts
unless specifically indicated.
Derailment/Left Roadway: A non-collision incident in which a transit vehicle leaves the rails or
road on which it travels (includes rollovers). All occurrences are reported, with no dollar
threshold.
Fatality: A transit-caused death confirmed within 30 days of a transit incident.
Fire: Uncontrolled combustion evidenced by flame and/or smoke requiring suppression by
equipment or personnel. No threshold; all fires are reported.
Grade Crossing: An intersection of highway roads, railroad tracks, or dedicated transit rail
tracks. Collisions at grade crossings involving transit vehicles apply only to light rail, commuter
rail, heavy rail, or trolleybus.
Injury: Any physical damage or harm to a person requiring medical treatment, or reported at the
time and place of occurrence.
Personal Casualty on Vehicle / Entering-Exiting / in Stations-Bus Stops: Incidents where a
person is hurt on a vehicle, boarding/alighting, or using a transit facility -- but NOT as a result of a
collision, derailment, or fire.
Suicide: A person intentionally attempting to end his or her own life; a subset of Collision with
People.

TRANSIT MODE DEFINITIONS
AG=Automated Guideway; CC=Cable Car; CR=Commuter Rail (urban passenger rail for local
short-distance travel, excludes heavy/light rail and intercity service); DR=Demand Response;
FB=Ferryboat; HR=Heavy Rail (electrically powered, usually third-rail, exclusive right-of-way,
longer trains/station spacing than light rail); IP=Inclined Plane; JT=Jitney; LR=Light Rail
(Streetcar; predominantly reserved but not always grade-separated right-of-way); MB=Motor Bus
(subdivided by agency fleet size into LMB [Large, 500+ buses], MMB [Medium], SMB [Small,
under 100 buses] -- this subdivision reflects operating environment differences and prevents
motor-bus statistics from dwarfing other modes in comparison charts; Trolleybus (TB) data are
combined with Motor Bus data); VP=Vanpool (8-18 seat vehicles, prearranged schedules,
pre-formed rider groups, drivers are commuters with little/no compensation beyond free
transportation).

SECURITY DATA HIGHLIGHTS (BUS, Part I offenses distribution)
Larceny/Theft was the most common Part I offense on buses (55.7% of all Part I offenses),
followed by Robbery (19.3%), Motor Vehicle Theft (10.0%), Burglary (8.2%), Aggravated Assault
(6.4%), Arson (0.2%), Forcible Rape (0.1%), and Homicide (0.1%). For bus, 63.9% of Part I
offenses occurred in vehicles, 17.8% in stations, and 18.3% elsewhere.

SECURITY DATA HIGHLIGHTS (RAIL, Part I offenses distribution)
Larceny/Theft was again dominant (51.2%), followed by Robbery (28.6%), Aggravated Assault
(14.9%), Motor Vehicle Theft (3.2%), Burglary (1.4%), Forcible Rape (0.5%), Arson (0.2%), and
Homicide (0.1%). For rail, most Part I offenses (53.2%) occurred "Other" locations (not
specifically in-vehicle or in-station), 34.0% in stations, and only 12.7% in vehicles -- a notably
different pattern from bus, where in-vehicle crime dominates.

Victims of Part I offenses on bus: 57.8% Patrons, 33.6% Employees, 6.7% Others, 1.9% Property.
Victims of Part I offenses on rail: 82.9% Patrons, 4.0% Employees, 4.7% Others, 8.4% Property.

PART II OFFENSES (ARRESTS) -- BUS: Fare Evasion was the single largest category at 58.4% of
all Part II arrests, followed by Disorderly Conduct (21.6%), Vandalism (4.6%), Drunkenness
(3.7%), Drug Abuse Violations (2.9%), Trespassing (5.7%), and smaller categories.
PART II OFFENSES (ARRESTS) -- RAIL: Fare Evasion was again the largest category (27.5%),
followed by Disorderly Conduct (19.8%), Trespassing (7.9%), Vandalism (21.9% - second
largest), Drug Abuse Violations (7.8%), and Other Assaults (5.5%). Notably, Vandalism was a much
larger share of rail arrests (21.9%) than bus arrests (4.6%).
