Preliminary Source-to-Question Mapping Notes (for questions.yaml drafting)
Domain 2: Municipal Transit Incidents
Accessed: 2026-09-19

This working document lists candidate facts, drawn from single source documents in this
corpus, suitable for the five required domain questions in questions.yaml -- specifically the
"at least two questions must depend on information found in only one source document"
requirement in the HW3 assignment.

CANDIDATE 1 (single-source: ntsb_har9602_fox_river_grove_raw_excerpt.txt)
Fact: The railroad's warning-time thumbwheel setting at the Fox River Grove crossing was
changed from 30 seconds to 25 seconds exactly two weeks before the accident (October 11,
1995), a change Union Pacific made "to ensure consistency... throughout the railroad's property."
This specific date and the "two weeks before" framing appears only in this one document among
the whole corpus.

CANDIDATE 2 (single-source: ntsb_rir2203_septa_spring_garden.txt)
Fact: Following the Spring Garden Station platform fatality, SEPTA announced a $3.6 million
SCOPE Program that increased outreach workers from 7 to 57. This specific dollar figure and
staffing change appears only in this document.

CANDIDATE 3 (single-source: ntsb_rar1002_fort_totten_raw_excerpt.txt)
Fact: WMATA's Advanced Information Management (AIM) system creates and deletes more
than 1,000 "ghost trains" per week as part of routine nuisance-alarm suppression, and track-
circuit-failed-occupied alarms occur at a rate of about 5,000 per week systemwide. These specific
weekly rates appear only in this document.

CANDIDATE 4 (single-source: ntsb_hab1810_nyc_transit_bus_pedestrian.txt)
Fact: The MTA bus driver involved in the 2016 NYC pedestrian fatality had been involved in five
prior transit bus crashes since April 2014 (at fault in at least two), plus four citizen reckless-
driving complaints and two speeding citations. This specific driving history appears only in this
document.

CANDIDATE 5 (single-source: fta_transit_bus_accident_investigations_2022.txt)
Fact: The FRA's Confidential Close Call Reporting System (C3RS) had, as of this report's
writing, 9 participating carriers, 18 active Peer Review Teams, and over 21,000 eligible frontline
railroad employees, and a 12-year evaluation found it reduced derailments at 3 of 4 demonstration
sites. These specific figures appear only in this document.

MULTI-SOURCE CANDIDATE (for a question requiring synthesis across documents)
Fact: The property-damage reporting threshold for non-rail transit collisions changed from a flat
$1,000 (per the 1997 SAMIS glossary) to $25,000 (per the 2026 FTA Safety & Security Policy
Manual) -- requiring the retriever to pull from BOTH fta_samis_1997_annual_report.txt AND
fta_ntd_safety_security_policy_manual_2026.txt (or the cross-reference summary in
fta_ntd_glossary_and_definitions_supplement.txt) to answer correctly. This is a good candidate
for demonstrating retrieval quality differences between chunking techniques, since a chunker that
fragments either source document poorly may retrieve only one side of the comparison.

ADVERSARIAL CANDIDATE (for the "confidently wrong retrieval" requirement)
A question asking specifically about the 2015 L'Enfant Plaza SMOKE incident's exact passenger
count or exact timeline is at risk of a chunker retrieving the WRONG WMATA incident (e.g.,
Fort Totten's passenger/casualty figures, or the Shady Grove fatality count) with high cosine
similarity, since all three documents share dense WMATA/Metrorail/Operations-Control-Center
vocabulary despite describing entirely different incidents years apart. This is a strong candidate
for the assignment's required "confidently scored retrieval that does not contain the answer"
demonstration.

UPDATE (after integrity check and re-verification pass)
Two additional single-source candidate facts, drawn from properly re-verified documents added
after the earlier integrity check removed unverified content:

CANDIDATE 6 (single-source: ntsb_rar1501_cta_ohare_fatigue.txt)
Fact: The CTA train operator's 72-hour sleep/wake history showed her longest single
uninterrupted sleep opportunity in the three days before the accident was only 6 hours 45
minutes, and she had worked her 12th consecutive calendar day (including two of her originally
scheduled days off, worked as overtime) at the time of the accident. This specific sleep-history
detail appears only in this document.

CANDIDATE 7 (single-source: ntsb_rar1101_miami_apm_verified.txt)
Fact: NTSB's own post-accident verification testing on the undamaged "north" train (five
separate braking/emergency-stop tests conducted December 7, 2008, without a jumper wire
installed) confirmed the overspeed/overshoot safety system worked exactly as designed in every
test. This specific test-by-test verification appears only in this document, and is a good candidate
for a question requiring the retriever to distinguish confirmed root-cause findings from merely
suspected ones.
