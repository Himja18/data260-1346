RAW SOURCE EXCERPT (verbatim facts, public domain U.S. government work)
NTSB/RAR-10/02 - Fort Totten Collision Report - Metrorail Upgrade Program Activities
Source: https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/accidentreports/reports/rar1002.pdf
Accessed: 2026-09-19
Supplements the other Fort Totten files in this corpus with detail on WMATA's ongoing
infrastructure upgrade programs at the time of the 2009 accident.

TRACTION POWER UPGRADE PROGRAM
At the time of the accident, WMATA had an ongoing Traction Power Upgrade Program intended
to increase electrical capacity across the Metrorail system to support more frequent train service
and newer railcars with higher power demands. This program involved replacing aging
substations and cabling systems that had been in service since the original 1970s construction of
the system.

TRACK CIRCUIT REPLACEMENT PROGRAM
WMATA also had a Track Circuit Replacement Program underway, intended to gradually replace
the original General Railway Signal (GRS) track circuit modules -- the same type of equipment
whose parasitic-oscillation failure caused the loss of train detection at Fort Totten -- with newer
Union Switch & Signal (US&S, later Ansaldo STS USA) equipment. This replacement program
had not yet reached the Fort Totten area at the time of the accident.

REPLACEMENT OF IMPEDANCE BONDS NEAR FORT TOTTEN
Separately, WMATA had recently performed work replacing impedance bonds (the components
at each end of a track circuit that transmit the detection signal into the rails) in the vicinity of the
Fort Totten accident location, as part of routine track circuit maintenance rather than the larger
replacement program. The investigation examined whether this recent maintenance work
introduced or failed to catch the parasitic oscillation condition that caused the accident, since the
enhanced circuit verification test developed after the 2005 Rosslyn near-miss -- which could have
caught this condition -- was not used during this maintenance work, consistent with the report's
broader finding that WMATA failed to institutionalize that enhanced test procedure systemwide.

PREVIOUS METRORAIL INCIDENT INVOLVING FAILURE OF TRAIN DETECTION
The report examined two near-collisions in 2005 near the Rosslyn Metrorail station in Virginia,
also resulting from a loss of train detection in the track circuit between the Foggy Bottom and
Rosslyn stations. Archived data reviewed during the Fort Totten investigation showed that the
Rosslyn track circuit had exhibited the same parasitic oscillation problem dating back to at least
1988 -- the earliest year for which data were available -- meaning the underlying equipment
defect had potentially been present, undetected, for over two decades before it caused a fatal
accident at a different location on the same system.

REVIEW OF ATC RECORDED DATA
Investigators reviewed archived Automatic Train Control (ATC) system data from the weeks and
months preceding the June 22, 2009 accident to determine whether the track circuit at location
B2-304 had shown any prior signs of intermittent malfunction before the fatal failure. This
retrospective data review was a key part of establishing that the parasitic oscillation condition,
once present, could persist undetected through normal operations because it produced a signal
that mimicked a valid, unoccupied-track reading rather than triggering any of the routine
track-circuit-failed-occupied or failed-vacant nuisance alarms that WMATA technicians were
accustomed to seeing and routinely dismissing as minor.

SAFETY OVERSIGHT OF TRANSIT OPERATIONS -- FEDERAL TRANSIT ADMINISTRATION
The report's "Safety Oversight of Transit Operations" section describes the Federal Transit
Administration's limited statutory authority over rail transit safety as it existed in 2009-2010,
prior to the later regulatory changes discussed in the 2015 L'Enfant Plaza oversight letter also in
this corpus. At the time of Fort Totten, FTA safety authority was exercised almost entirely
through the State Safety Oversight Agency (SSOA) framework under 49 CFR Part 659, with no
direct FTA inspection or enforcement authority over WMATA itself -- the same underlying
structural weakness the NTSB would revisit in far greater depth five years later following the
L'Enfant Plaza incident.
