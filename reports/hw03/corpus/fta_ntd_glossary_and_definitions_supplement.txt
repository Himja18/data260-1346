National Transit Database (NTD) - Supplementary Glossary and Cross-Reference Notes
Compiled from FTA NTD reporting manuals (Safety & Security Policy Manual 2026,
National Transit Summaries & Trends 2021) and SAMIS Annual Report glossary sections.
Source: https://www.transit.dot.gov (multiple FTA NTD publications, see other corpus files
for individual document URLs and access dates).
Accessed: 2026-09-19
This file consolidates cross-cutting terminology used across the FTA/NTD documents in this
corpus, to aid consistent interpretation of Domain 2 (Municipal Transit Incidents) source material.

REPORTING TIER DEFINITIONS (NTD)
Full Reporters: Transit agencies that submit complete Safety and Security (S&S) data on all
required forms (S&S-20, S&S-30, S&S-40, S&S-50).
Reduced Reporters: Urban transit agencies with 30 vehicles or fewer in maximum service,
eligible (since FY 2011) to skip certain reporting categories including passenger miles,
mode-specific costs, employee counts, maintenance performance, energy consumption, monthly
ridership, and safety data. Reduced Reporters instead report only annual totals of major events,
fatalities, and injuries for the year rather than event-by-event detail.
Rural Reporters: Recipients/beneficiaries of FTA's Section 5311 Rural Areas Formula Program;
report to the rural module of the NTD, generally through their state (states file rural reports on
behalf of subrecipient rural agencies, who do not report directly).
Build Reporters / Plan Reporters: Agencies receiving federal funding for constructing transit
infrastructure or for planning/programming, but not yet operating revenue service.
Voluntary Reporters: Public or private transit systems that are not required to report to the NTD
but choose to do so anyway, and must meet the same obligations as mandatory reporters once they
opt in.

MODE ABBREVIATIONS USED ACROSS NTD DOCUMENTS (consolidated)
AG = Automated Guideway; AR = Alaska Railroad; CC = Cable Car; CR = Commuter Rail;
DR = Demand Response; FB = Ferryboat; HR = Heavy Rail; IP = Inclined Plane; JT = Jitney;
LR = Light Rail; MB = Motor Bus (further subdivided in older SAMIS-era reports into LMB/
MMB/SMB by operating agency's fleet size: Large 500+, Medium, Small <100 -- NOT by
physical vehicle size); MG = Monorail/Automated Guideway; PB = Publico (unique to Puerto
Rico); RB = Bus Rapid Transit; SR = Streetcar Rail; TB = Trolleybus (data combined with Motor
Bus in SAMIS-era reporting); TN = Transportation Network Company; TR = Aerial Tramway;
VP = Vanpool; YR = Hybrid Rail.

KEY METRICS USED ACROSS FTA STATISTICAL REPORTS
UPT (Unlinked Passenger Trips): a count of boardings; a single journey involving a transfer
between vehicles counts as multiple UPT.
PMT (Passenger Miles Traveled): total miles traveled by all passengers combined (UPT
multiplied by average trip distance, roughly).
VRM (Vehicle Revenue Miles) / VRH (Vehicle Revenue Hours): miles/hours a vehicle travels
while in revenue service, available to carry passengers (excludes deadhead/maintenance travel).
VOMS (Vehicles Operated in Maximum Service): the peak number of vehicles an agency has in
service at once, typically during rush hour.
Farebox Recovery Ratio: the percentage of operating costs covered by passenger fare revenue.

CROSS-DOCUMENT TERMINOLOGY RECONCILIATION -- "COLLISION"
The FTA's current (2026) Safety & Security Policy Manual defines a collision broadly as any
vehicle/vessel accident involving impact with another vehicle, a person, an animal, an object, a
rail vehicle, a vessel, or a dock, and requires separate rail vs. non-rail reportability thresholds. The
older 1997 SAMIS report used a narrower, threshold-based definition: "An incident in which a
transit vehicle strikes or is struck by another vehicle... Reports are made if the accident results in
death, injury, or property damage over $1,000" -- a flat $1,000 threshold, contrasted with the 2026
manual's $25,000 non-rail property-damage threshold (adjusted upward over nearly 30 years,
partly reflecting inflation and partly a policy decision to focus federal reporting on more serious
events). Researchers comparing incident counts across eras (e.g., 1997 SAMIS vs. 2021 NTST or
the 2026 manual) should account for this definitional and threshold drift rather than assuming
directly comparable "collision" counts.

CROSS-DOCUMENT NOTE -- FATALITY DEFINITION CONSISTENCY
All FTA/NTD documents in this corpus consistently define a reportable fatality as a death
confirmed within 30 days of the transit-related event, explicitly excluding deaths from illness,
natural causes, or drug overdose unrelated to the event itself. This definition has remained stable
from the 1997 SAMIS glossary through the 2026 Policy Manual, unlike the property-damage
threshold, which has changed substantially.

CROSS-DOCUMENT NOTE -- OVERSIGHT AGENCY ACRONYMS
FTA = Federal Transit Administration (federal). SSOA = State Safety Oversight Agency (state-level,
required under 49 CFR Part 659 for each state with a non-FRA-regulated rail fixed-guideway
system). TOC = Tri-State Oversight Committee (WMATA's specific SSOA, covering Virginia,
Maryland, and D.C. jointly -- the only multi-jurisdictional SSOA structured this way in the
country as of the mid-2010s). FRA = Federal Railroad Administration (regulates commuter rail
and other "commuter authority" systems under 45 USC 1104, with much stronger inspection and
enforcement powers than the FTA/SSOA framework governing rapid transit).
