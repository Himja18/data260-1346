NTSB/HAR-01/03
HIGHWAY ACCIDENT REPORT
Collision of CSXT Freight Train and Murray County School District School Bus at
Railroad/Highway Grade Crossing, Conasauga, Tennessee, March 28, 2000
Source: https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/HAR0103.pdf
Accessed: 2026-09-19

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
On March 28, 2000, about 6:40 a.m. (sunrise was at 6:33 a.m.), a CSX Transportation, Inc.
(CSXT), freight train traveling 51 mph struck the passenger side of a Murray County, Georgia,
School District school bus at a railroad/highway grade crossing near Conasauga, Tennessee. The
accident occurred as the school bus was crossing the tracks at a speed of approximately 15 mph.
During the accident sequence, the driver and three children were ejected. Two ejected passengers
received serious injuries and one was fatally injured. The driver, who had been wearing a
lap/shoulder belt that broke during the crash sequence, received minor injuries. Of the four
passengers who remained inside the bus, two were fatally injured, one sustained serious injuries,
and one, who was restrained by a lap belt, received minor injuries. The two train crewmembers
were not injured.

The National Transportation Safety Board determines that the probable cause of the collision
was the school bus driver's failure to stop before traversing the railroad/highway grade crossing.
Contributing to the accident was the Murray County, Georgia, School District's failure to monitor
busdriver performance and its lack of school bus route planning to identify hazards on school bus
routes and to eliminate the necessity of crossing railroad tracks. Contributing to the injuries of
the school bus passengers were incomplete compartmentalization and a lack of energy-absorbing
material on interior surfaces.

ACCIDENT NARRATIVE
The school bus was equipped with video recording equipment to monitor passenger behavior.
Examination of the videotapes found on the bus showed that the school bus did not stop, as
required, before attempting to cross the railroad tracks on the day of the accident, nor had it
stopped at this crossing on eight previous occasions in the two weeks preceding the accident.
Investigators identified 11 crossings on the videotape as the Liberty Church Road crossing: the
bus stopped only twice (both times when another vehicle was present behind it).

In a postaccident interview, the busdriver stated that she came to a complete stop, looked left
then right, opened and closed the door, and proceeded -- but the videotape contradicted this
account. The train crew (engineer and conductor) saw the bus approaching and yelled; the
engineer held the horn down continuously and placed the train in emergency braking, but the bus
did not stop. The train struck the bus on the passenger side between the axles.

INJURIES
Table: Driver 1 minor injury; Train crew 0 injuries (2 total, both uninjured); Bus passengers: 3
fatal, 3 serious, 1 minor (7 total). Overall total across all persons: 10.

Two fatally injured passengers were seated in the area of impact; a third fatally injured
passenger, seated in the last row, was ejected. The bus driver's lap/shoulder belt broke during the
accident, ejecting her; she sustained a fractured nose and lacerations.

TOXICOLOGICAL TESTS
The busdriver's blood and urine tested negative for alcohol and illicit drugs, but positive for
pseudoephedrine, ephedrine, and phenylpropanolamine -- common ingredients in over-the-counter
cold medications and weight-loss products, none of which typically cause impairment. The train
engineer and conductor volunteered for testing (not required by regulation after a grade-crossing
collision) and were negative for drugs and alcohol.

SCHOOL BUS DRIVER
The 34-year-old driver was hired as a full-time substitute busdriver on May 11, 1999, and became
a full-time driver at the start of the 1999-2000 school year. She held a valid Georgia CDL with a
passenger endorsement. She attended a required annual 8-hour recertification training session on
August 12, 1999, covering grade-crossing procedures, and had previously (with a different
employer) been reprimanded for talking during a similar training session.

SIGHT DISTANCE AND AUDIBILITY
Safety Board investigators determined that when the bus was stopped 15 feet from the nearest
rail, the driver could see the train when it was 1,268 feet from the crossing -- well beyond the
960 feet of sight distance the bus would have needed (per AASHTO guidance) to safely cross
from a stop given a 60-mph train. Audibility tests found that with the bus's radio on and door
closed (the conditions at the time of the accident), the train horn was only 5 decibels above
ambient noise at 1,268 feet -- barely detectable -- and was not audible on the accident videotape's
audio track at all. With the radio off and door open, the horn was 26 decibels above ambient
noise, comfortably audible.

ACCIDENT SIMULATION
Using vehicle dynamics and occupant kinematics simulation (MADYMO), Safety Board staff
calculated peak accelerations during the initial lateral impact of 30 Gs at the bus's center of
gravity, 39 Gs at the last row of seats, and 31 Gs at the first row, with peak angular acceleration of
about 2,500 degrees/second/second, driven by the bus's clockwise rotation and pivoting about the
front axle.

RAILROAD/HIGHWAY GRADE CROSSING SAFETY -- PASSIVE CROSSINGS
In an earlier 1998 safety study, the Safety Board investigated 60 accidents at passive grade
crossings (December 1995-August 1996) and found driver error was the primary cause in 49 of
60 cases. Of the 11 passive crossings in Polk County and 8 in Murray County examined by
investigators, only 3 in Murray County had stop signs; none in Polk County did. The Liberty
Church Road crossing had not been designated for improvement before the accident; it was
upgraded to an active crossing with gates and lights on September 9, 2000, following the
accident.

An earlier locomotive engineer had reported to investigators that 11 days before the accident, on
March 17, 2000, he witnessed the same school bus cross the tracks without stopping, but did not
report it at the time because he could not read the bus's identifying numbers.

RELATIONSHIP TO THE 1995 FOX RIVER GROVE ACCIDENT
This report references NTSB/HAR-96/02, the 1995 Fox River Grove, Illinois, accident. Following
that accident, the Safety Board had recommended (H-96-52, H-96-53) that the National
Association of State Directors of Pupil Transportation Services (NASDPTS) encourage members
to develop school bus route hazard identification programs and monitor busdriver performance,
and to consider grade-crossing accident histories when establishing routes. It had also
recommended (H-96-50, H-96-51) guidelines on radio speaker placement in school buses and
disabling speakers near the driver's head -- both closed "Acceptable Alternate Action" in 1999
after a NASDPTS survey found most States had prohibited or were prohibiting radio speakers in
the driver's compartment. Despite this, the bus involved in the Conasauga accident still had an
active radio/speaker near the driver at the time of the accident.

PROBABLE CAUSE
The National Transportation Safety Board determines that the probable cause of the collision
was the school bus driver's failure to stop before traversing the railroad/highway grade crossing.
Contributing to the accident was the Murray County, Georgia, School District's failure to monitor
busdriver performance and its lack of route planning to identify hazards. Contributing to the
severity of injuries was incomplete compartmentalization and a lack of energy-absorbing material
on interior bus surfaces.

RECOMMENDATIONS
The Safety Board made recommendations to the States, the National Highway Traffic Safety
Administration, the Federal Highway Administration, the Georgia Department of Education, the
National Association of State Directors of Pupil Transportation Services, and school bus
manufacturers, and reiterated a recommendation to the U.S. Department of Transportation.
